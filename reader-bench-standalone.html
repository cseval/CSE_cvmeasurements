What to do

Run this on whatever machine the reader is plugged into. A laptop is fine — you are testing the reader, not the iPad.

  1. 1Plug the reader in and wait for the light. Dim blue means armed and waiting.
  2. 2Tap one bracelet. The reader beeps once, long. This panel should turn green within a second, with no clicking.
  3. 3Tap ten different bracelets in a row. Do not touch the screen between taps. All ten pips must light.
  4. 4Tap the same bracelet twice quickly. The second one should do nothing and the reader should give two short beeps. That is the 3 second cooldown working.
  5. 5Wait four seconds, tap it again. Now it should register as a repeat, which is a legitimate second measurement.
  6. 6Copy the log and send it over. The character counts tell us which chip family your bracelets actually are.

What counts as a problem

Nothing happens at all — the reader is not being seen as a keyboard, or it is not powered. Try it in a plain text editor: if nothing types there either, it is the hardware, not this page.

An on-screen keyboard pops up — on an iPad that means the reader is not connected. With a real keyboard attached, iOS hides the software one.

Some taps in the ten miss — this is the failure worth catching. It usually looks like a flaky reader and is usually the page losing focus. The log's focus-loss counter tells the two apart.

Odd character counts — a truncated read. The bracelet left the field too early. Hold it flat until the beep.

Running from a file

On an iPad or iPhone, tap the share button and choose Add to Home Screen. It opens full screen after that, with no browser bars, which is closer to how the station will actually look. The page needs no internet connection to work.
